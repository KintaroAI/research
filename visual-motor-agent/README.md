# Visual–Motor Agent

> A small, randomly initialized agent learns to operate a mouse from pixels alone, with no labels,
> coordinates, DOM, demonstrations or distance shaping. What does it learn about its own actions
> without being reinforced for it?

## Current state (2026-11-27)

The project began with the click task described below (vm-00001 to vm-00018). Since vm-00019 it studies one
**universal model** living **one life**: a causal Transformer in which every token (image patch, body value, move)
predicts the next one through the same head and loss, taking one gradient step per tick on its own replayed
experience. What to seek enters as one more predicted body value, the meal label, which a one-step instinct reads to
choose the move.

- **The default agent** (`dev/configs/U_agent.json`, vm-00101's TT, the default since 2026-11-22):
  - the world: 128 x 128 with 2 foods, 8 poisons and 32 moving dots, seen through a 64 x 64 window centered on the
    body, which moves with inertia and eats by touch;
  - the model: four quarter transformers of 8 layers, a context of 8 ticks, about 2.2M parameters each;
  - the label: a meal is worth 1, carried back in time with γ .95 and re-labeled at every replay (vm-00097 to
    vm-00100);
  - the move: the best of 16 moves sampled from the model's own move prediction at temperature 2, scored by the
    predicted label.
- **Where it stands:**
  - food world: 71.6 food and 8.3 poison per 1k ticks at 40–60k (12 seeds, vm-00101);
  - Breakout, the model unchanged: .68 of the balls returned at 50–60k against a random paddle's .235 (vm-00103), and
    .95 after 1M ticks in the court with bricks (vm-00109).
- **Against standard learners on the same pixels, signal and 60k ticks** (vm-00108, seeds 1–6):

  | PPO, one body | .20 | 30.3 and 7.1 |

- **Known limits:**
  - **Memory:** nothing lasts beyond the 8-tick context. A 32-tick context (vm-00092), a state carried across windows
    (vm-00093) and fast weights (vm-00094) all failed to give one.
  - **Horizon:** the label is right to about 10–15 ticks before a meal. What pays later is not learned: no preference
    for better bricks in vm-00104, vm-00107, vm-00109's 1M ticks, or at γ .997 (vm-00111).
  - **Imagination:** the model's own dream is noise by 4 ticks, so looking ahead through it hurts (vm-00114).
  - **The acting rule** (task 00123): in Breakout more replay speeds the start but levels off at .73 (vm-00110), and
    not because the candidates narrow (vm-00113). The trained agent's label knows where the paddle should be but barely
    orders the candidate moves (right about six times in ten), so the paddle dithers (vm-00116). The move token
    trained as a policy-gradient actor in place of the instinct narrows to a near-fixed move within 20k ticks and
    learns neither world (vm-00115). A move decided every 4 ticks and held is ordered better by the label (.69
    against .62) and still plays a little worse (−.065 ± .053 hit share), because one decision in 4 ticks corrects
    less than four (vm-00118). Within 8 px of the catching zone the move goes toward the ball half the time, held or
    not.

    the old world): a food-world past helps in Breakout (+.145 ± .031 hit share at 10–30k) and a Breakout past hurts

The sections from Objective to Success criteria are the project's starting point (2026-10-23).

## Objective

Build a pixels-to-mouse agent: a CNN encoder, a causal Transformer, and policy, value and
action-conditioned prediction heads. Use it to study whether self-supervised prediction and
internal drives give it sensorimotor knowledge (what its moves and clicks do) before, or instead
of, reward. Then test whether that knowledge helps it learn the task.

## Hypothesis

1. A forward model trained only to predict the next frame from (frame, action) learns the meaning
   of its motor API without any reward:
   - dx/dy move the cursor;
   - a click while the cursor is over the circle makes the circle disappear;
   - that same click relieves hunger.
2. Sharing that representation with the policy (auxiliary loss), or pre-training it by babbling,
   makes the sparse-reward task learnable faster.

## Success criteria

- Counterfactual action-conditioned predictions beat chance and trivial baselines (copy last
  frame, action-agnostic predictor) on held-out states. Metric: `analyze_dynamics.py`.
- The click contingency is specific: the circle vanishes when the click is inside it, not outside.
- PPO arms compared on steps-to-success and sample efficiency, over seeds, against the
  random-policy baseline (73.5% success at radius 12–16, 39% at radius 4–8).

## Setup

- **Hardware:** QEMU VM, 128 vCPUs (SSE4.2 only, no AVX), 251 GB RAM; an RTX 3090 since 2026-10-24 and an RTX 4090
  since 2026-10-25 (24 GB each). Lifetimes run on the GPUs (`libvmcuda.so`, `online.device=cuda`), several per card
  under CUDA MPS.
- **Model:** C++17 + OpenMP + hand-written SSE GEMM, verified against a float64 PyTorch reference
  (gradients match to about 1e-7 relative error); the CUDA port (`dev/src/vm_cuda.cu`) is checked against both.
- **Worlds:** Python/numpy: the click world (with an HTML/JS replica that renders pixel-identical frames), the touch
  and food worlds, text, a MuJoCo car, Breakout.
- **Throughput:** PPO on the CPU about 2k env-steps/s per run, two runs in parallel. A 60k-tick life of the default
  agent takes about 3 h in Breakout and 5.5 h in the food world with 6 lives per card (vm-00108).
- **Outputs:** `~/data/research/visual-motor-agent/exp_NNNNN/`, not in the repo.

## Open Questions

**Now (2026-11-27).** The research plan is [`ARCHITECTURE.md`](ARCHITECTURE.md), section 13. What comes first is its
section 15 (the owner, 2026-11-26): the world model, its dream and its memory, measured by numbers that do not depend
on the model's parts. The first table of those numbers is vm-00119; on it one transformer on the whole frame predicts better than the four (vm-00120), and training on its own dreamed frames makes the dream hold where the life knows the things (vm-00121: the court's ball, not the disc world's new discs). A life of 200k ticks keeps improving on what it knows and not on new discs (vm-00122, one seed an arm). By the owner's word of 2026-11-27 a change is now tried on 1 to 2 seeds an arm; many seeds wait until something is worth confirming. The acting rule and rehearsal, below, wait for that. The five challenges stand as follows:

- **One set of weights, many worlds.** The same code runs the food worlds, text, a MuJoCo car and Breakout, each in
  its own life. The first step is run (vm-00117): the weights of a life in one world start a life in the other.
  Transfer is one-sided (the food world helps Breakout, Breakout hurts the food world), the old world's play is
  forgotten within 10k ticks, and coming back the food-world past still counts while the Breakout past does not
  show. Open: rehearsal (one process, one memory across both worlds), then the flexible layer; a life that passes
  through several worlds and comes back with one memory has not been run.
- **Learning from one event.** Not tried here.
- **Abstraction over time.** Nothing lasts beyond the 8-tick context (vm-00091 to vm-00094), and the label reaches
  about 15 ticks (vm-00104, vm-00107, vm-00109, vm-00111). A task that a memoryless agent cannot solve, with a valid
  control, is still missing (vm-00057's delayed cue was not valid).
- **Where goals come from.** A rule outside the network still chooses the move. Task 00123 asks whether a trained
  actor should replace it: the dream lookahead failed (vm-00114), and so did a policy-gradient actor on real windows
  (vm-00115); a critic trained on dreams is untried. Action repeat (the owner, 2026-11-17, `ARCHITECTURE.md`
  section 9) improved the label's order of the moves but not the play (vm-00118). What is left of task 00123 are the
  arms that teach the label what a move does: an advantage-style target, and labels for moves not made.
- **Scale.** Worlds up to 128 x 128, up to 64 transformers in one body, up to 8.6M parameters in one transformer,
  lives up to 1M ticks. Whether quality holds beyond that is not measured.

Also open: why damage and outages lower poison on clean input (vm-00074, vm-00079); what makes the model's dream
hold past 2–3 ticks (self-forcing kept it truer in vm-00032; learned discrete tokens are untried), which section 15
takes up first, after the measures.

**From the first experiments (vm-00001 to vm-00010):**

- **Partly answered (vm-00001).**
  - A reward-free predictor needs about 0.5M (movement) to 1M (click/hunger contingencies) random-
    policy steps, about 4–8k updates.
  - It needs U-Net skips, change prediction, nonlinear action conditioning and a high update rate.
  - Inverse dynamics speeds it up 2–4×.
- **Partly answered.** Babbling pre-training transfers: PPO is 3.3–3.5× faster (2 seeds). Open: how much of the gain comes from the encoder (cursor localization) vs the
  decoder/latent, and does it hold for small targets?
- **Answered: yes (vm-00002).** The learned model can *act* without reward, by planning to minimize
  predicted hunger. A latent dynamics model trained during babbling supports a CEM planner: 89% hits
  against 23% for the control. Open: why the planner falls short of 100% (it exploits model errors at
  the circle's edge), and whether learning from its own failed clicks closes the gap. **It does**
  (vm-00004, two babblers): 100% hits in a median of 5–6 steps.
- At radius 12–16 the task is close to non-sparse for random clicking. At radius 4–8, PPO stays at
  the random level for about 0.6M steps before learning (vm-00001 R5). Which conditions make
  exploration genuinely hard (smaller targets, short episodes, click cost)?
- Drives as inputs/costs vs exploration bonuses (vm-00003, one seed per arm): the hunger cost speeds
  up PPO, and bonuses do not. More seeds are needed.
- **Partly answered: moving the decision into the network.** In vm-00002 to vm-00005, the network only
  predicts. The goal (lower predicted hunger, summed over 12 imagined steps) and the search (CEM)
  are hand-written code outside it (`dev/plan.py`). A pure predictor has no preference: in vm-00006
  A, its own moves just drift. Ideas to try:
  1. **Planner as teacher (habit).** Run vm-00005's L with `ppo.move_coef=1`, so the policy head
     also learns to predict the planner's executed moves. Then evaluate the head's own moves without
     the planner (`online.py --eval`, as in vm-00006). Search would then be needed only while
     learning. **Done in vm-00009:** a detached linear head acts alone at 0.80–0.96 test success at 30k
     (its planners 0.88–0.95), in 26–44 steps against the planner's 8–14. The babbled moves enter the move
     loss. The move loss must not train the shared encoder: through it, it erodes the features. Open:
     does the habit hold once the planner is switched off?
  2. **Hindsight hunger input, no search** (vm-00006 A+H). Hits are rare (1–4 per 1,000 steps), so
     only a few percent of the training steps carry the signal. **Tried in vm-00007:** memory gated
     by relief raises the labelled share to 9–14%, but the agent still does not aim, because its
     features lose the cursor during training. Next: stop the move loss's gradient at the policy
     head, or start from a world model learned by babbling or by L. **Done in vm-00008:** detached, the
     features hold; with one hidden layer in the head, the command steers it, and the basic agent aims
     (0.63–0.82 at 50k against 0.45 at birth), without reward or planner. Moving dots delay it by
     20k–30k steps (vm-00010).
